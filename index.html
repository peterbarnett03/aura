FOR IPHONE

A tracker built for palindromic rheumatism.

Flares arrive, move between joints and resolve completely. Aura is built around that, not around a chronic ache that stays put — so logging one takes under a minute, and months of them add up to something you can show a rheumatologist.

Coming to the App Store

Counts you can check, not predictions

Nothing here forecasts a flare, because nothing can. What the app does instead is keep an honest tally: which joints, how often, what you tagged alongside it, and what the weather was doing on the days you were fine as well as the days you weren't.

It won't tell you more than it knows

Two flares and one of them tagged "alcohol" is not a fifty per cent correlation, so Aura doesn't call it one. Percentages stay hidden until there is a sample worth quoting, the weather comparison waits for enough days in each pressure band, and the summary you hand to a clinician reports counts rather than conclusions.

Aura is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent anything, and nothing it shows is medical advice. Any pattern it reports is descriptive only.

  • No account, no backend

    There is nothing to sign up for. Your records live in a database on your phone, and there is no server holding a copy.

  • Log a flare in under a minute

    Joints, pain, symmetry, what you think set it off. Start it when it begins, mark it resolved when it goes.

  • Apple Health, read only

    Sleep, heart rate and HRV shown alongside your flare history. Aura reads; it never writes, and never shares.

  • A summary for your appointment

    Export a PDF of the period your rheumatologist asked about, with the counts laid out and no interpretation added.

  • New research, as it appears

    Palindromic rheumatism papers from PubMed Central, on a condition most people have to go looking for news about.

  • No ads, no analytics

    No advertising network, no tracking SDK, no third-party libraries at all. Read the policy.
